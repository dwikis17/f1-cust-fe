## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** The execution is coherent, but the art direction still reads as plausibly AI-generated. It leans heavily on the saturated “motorsport = black/orange + technical mono + oversized uppercase + telemetry” lane. Tiny tracked eyebrows recur across nearly every surface; the hero’s `01 — 03` telemetry treatment implies a carousel that does not exist; and pseudo-technical phrases such as “Selected works” and “Engineering brief” add atmosphere without helping a purchase decision. Generated fictional racing imagery also sits uneasily beside “Official merchandise” and McLaren-specific claims.

The restraint is real: there is no glassmorphism, gradient text, giant rounded card system, decorative grid, or ghost-card styling. The problem is category-reflex art direction and ornamental semantics, not random inconsistency.

## Overall Impression

The storefront looks much more finished than it behaves. Its biggest opportunity is to shift effort from motorsport atmosphere into commerce trust and wayfinding: fewer taxonomy choices, clearer literal language, functioning reassurance links, and stronger status feedback would make the brand feel more premium than another layer of telemetry styling.

## What’s Working

1. **A coherent visual system.** The near-black, white, and orange palette; Space Grotesk display type; mono utility labels; square controls; button language; and shared spacing make the storefront feel intentionally designed rather than stitched together.

2. **Strong responsive structure.** Product grids collapse logically, filters become a mobile disclosure, cart and checkout columns stack, gallery thumbnails reorient, and the mobile hero asset preserves the subject. The responsive behavior changes structure rather than simply shrinking text.

3. **Good state foundations.** Collection loading/error/empty states, cart loading/empty/unavailable states, active-filter chips, query preservation, visible focus styling, semantic landmarks, labeled forms, and reduced-motion handling show production awareness.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] False and dead affordances undermine store trust

**Why it matters:** The inert Account control and `#` footer links are credibility checks, not peripheral details. First-time and checkout-minded users use support, returns, legal, and account surfaces to decide whether the store is safe. Generated imagery beside “Official” claims compounds the uncertainty.

**Fix:** Remove unavailable controls or give them honest “coming soon” treatment. Build real support/legal destinations, add a cart count and confirmation state, and put concrete licensing, authenticity, returns, and fulfillment proof near product and cart decisions.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] Navigation and collections create a wall of options

**Why it matters:** The desktop navigation exposes roughly 11 teams and 22 drivers; mobile reveals the full taxonomy; `/collections` repeats about 33 destinations before search and the product grid. Users must learn the catalog architecture before they can shop.

**Fix:** Use one labeled Shop disclosure with grouped Teams, Drivers, and Product Types. Surface 4–6 popular destinations plus search, then progressively reveal “View all.” Give `/collections` one dominant job: curated directory or product index, not both at equal weight.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Utility typography and mobile controls are too small

**Why it matters:** Navigation, footer, filters, metadata, and actions frequently use 8–10px mono text with wide tracking. Locale buttons and several header controls do not consistently guarantee a 44px target, while Search disappears below 600px. This trades usability for technical character.

**Fix:** Raise utility text to an effective 11–12px minimum, reduce tracking, ensure every header/locale/menu target is at least 44×44px, and keep Search available in the mobile header or menu.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### [P2] Decorative telemetry implies behavior that is not present

**Why it matters:** The hero displays `01` and `03` around an animated progress line without slides, controls, or alternate states. It is both a misleading affordance and a conspicuous category/AI tell.

**Fix:** Implement a real accessible three-story campaign carousel with pause and state semantics, or remove the index. Keep motion where it communicates loading, hover, selection, or navigation.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable quieter`

### [P2] Commerce status and recovery are incomplete

**Why it matters:** Filter and sort submissions lack pending feedback, navigation has no active state, cart removal has no undo, the bag has no quantity badge, and disabled pagination remains link markup. Users cannot always tell what changed or safely reverse it.

**Fix:** Add pending/result announcements, active navigation, cart count and add/remove confirmation, a timed Undo after removal, and non-interactive pagination elements at boundaries.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish`

## Cognitive Load

**Five of eight checklist items fail: high cognitive load.** Grouping, visual hierarchy, and working-memory support are good. Single focus, chunking, one-thing-at-a-time, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure fail because navigation and `/collections` expose the whole taxonomy alongside search, products, filters, and sort.

Decision points exceeding four visible options include the 11-team navigation group, 22-driver group, the full mobile taxonomy, the 33-link directory, seven sort options, and the 11-team homepage strip.

## Emotional Journey

- **Entry peak:** Night racing imagery, assertive type, and the orange accent create energy and a premium first impression.
- **Discovery valley:** A deep collection taxonomy opens into visibly sparse inventory, making the catalog feel broader than it is.
- **Trust valley:** Generated imagery, official-merchandise language, an inert Account control, and dead support/legal links weaken confidence when users look for proof.
- **Cart reassurance:** Clear variants, quantities, subtotal, shipping expectations, and unavailable-stock blocking reduce anxiety.
- **Weak ending:** The footer should close with shipping, returns, contact, authenticity, and legal reassurance, but its destinations do not work.

## Minor Observations

- “View campaign” jumps to an editorial block rather than a campaign experience.
- Collection loading uses one large rectangle instead of a grid-shaped skeleton.

## Questions to Consider

- What if Shop opened a searchable, prioritized taxonomy rather than every team and driver?
- Is `01 — 03` meant to become a real campaign sequence? If not, what user purpose does it serve?
- What concrete proof would make “Official merchandise” credible before checkout?
- Should `/collections` be primarily a directory or a product index?
- What must the footer say—and actually link to—for a first-time buyer to trust a multimillion-rupiah order?
- Could the motorsport identity survive with half as much mono uppercase telemetry and twice as much product-specific information?
